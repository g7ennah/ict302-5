**Background**

The Xbox Series X was released in November 2020 as the most powerful console in Microsoft’s lineup. Aimed at both casual and hardcore gamers, it delivers high-speed gaming through its custom AMD Zen 2 CPU and RDNA 2 GPU, enabling ray tracing and consistent high frame rates. It’s designed for enhanced performance in demanding titles, fast game switching through Quick Resume, and quiet operation despite its power.

The console supports up to 4K gaming at 120Hz, features a 1TB NVMe SSD, and provides excellent backward compatibility with thousands of games from previous Xbox generations. Its vertical “tower-style” design maximizes airflow, while the top-mounted cooling system pushes heat upward through a circular exhaust vent.

Most users praise the Series X for its speed, graphics, and reliability. There have been no major widespread hardware recalls, though some users have reported disc drive noise or minor power supply failures in early units—not uncommon for high-performance electronics.

The Xbox Series X was followed by mid-generation refreshes offering expanded storage and cosmetic variations, but the original model remains a top-tier console for performance and longevity.

**Identification**

The Xbox Series X features a tall, rectangular tower design with a matte black finish. The top of the console includes a distinctive circular exhaust vent with green accenting visible beneath the grille. On the front panel, you’ll find:

A power button with the Xbox logo

A USB-A port

The controller sync button

A disc slot (optical drive models only)

The model and serial number are printed on a label near the rear ports. The back of the console includes a unique grouping of ports arranged vertically for easy access.

All official Xbox Series X models are black.
