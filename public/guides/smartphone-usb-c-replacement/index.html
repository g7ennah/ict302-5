Smartphone USB-C Charging Port Replacement

Garret

November 20, 2025

The Xbox Series X is Microsoft’s flagship gaming console, built for high-performance gameplay, fast load times, and next-gen graphical capabilities. As part of the fourth generation of Xbox consoles, it’s known for its powerful hardware, durable chassis, and efficient cooling system designed for long gaming sessions.

Difficulty Level: ⭐⭐⭐/5

Time Required: 40-60 minutes

Background: Over time, USB-C charging ports can wear out, fill with debris, or become loose from repeated cable insertion. When the phone only charges at certain angles, doesn’t connect to a computer, or stops fast charging, replacing the charging port assembly can restore full functionality.

Identification: This guide is written for a generic Android smartphone with a USB-C charging port on the bottom edge and a separate charging daughterboard (common on many mid-range phones). Confirm your phone: Has a USB-C port centered on the bottom frame. Uses a plastic back or a removable midframe after lifting the display or back cover.

Overview: Open the phone, disconnect and remove the charging daughterboard, and install a new USB-C port assembly.

Tools Required:

~Small Phillips screwdriver
~Plastic opening picks
~Suction cup
~Plastic spudger
~Tweezers
~Heat gun or hairdryer

Parts Needed:

~Replacement USB-C charging port daughterboard (compatible with your phone model)
~Replacement adhesive for back cover or display (if needed)

⚠️Safety Warning⚠️: Lithium-ion batteries can be dangerous if punctured. Do not pry directly under the battery with metal tools, and avoid bending the battery when lifting components.

Step 1 — Power Off and Remove SIM Tray

What to do: Shut down the phone completely. Use the SIM eject tool or a small paper clip to remove the SIM tray so it doesn’t get stuck when opening the device.

Step 2 — Soften the Back Cover Adhesive

What to do: If your phone opens from the back, gently heat the edges of the back cover with a heat gun or hairdryer for 1–2 minutes, moving constantly. The goal is to soften the adhesive, not to overheat the phone.

Step 3 — Lift the Back Cover

What to do: Apply a suction cup near the bottom edge and pull up slightly to create a small gap. Insert a plastic pick into the gap and carefully slide it around the perimeter to release the adhesive. Take your time around corners and near buttons.

Step 4 — Disconnect the Battery

What to do: Remove any screws holding the plastic midframe or shield in place. Lift the plastic cover with a spudger. Locate the battery connector on the main board and gently pry it straight up with a plastic spudger to cut power to the system.

Step 5 — Locate and Remove the Charging Daughterboard

What to do: At the bottom of the phone, locate the small board that contains the USB-C port. Disconnect any ribbon cables leading to the charging board (from the main board and possibly from the loudspeaker). Remove the screws securing the loudspeaker assembly or plastic frame over the board. Lift out the loudspeaker module if needed to access the board fully. Remove the screws holding the charging board and gently lift it out with tweezers.

Step 6 — Install the New Charging Port Board

What to do: Place the new daughterboard into the same position, aligning the USB-C port in the frame cutout. Reinstall any screws that secure the board. Reconnect all ribbon cables exactly as they were, ensuring each connector clicks into place.

Step 7 — Reconnect Battery and Test

What to do: Reconnect the battery connector to the main board. Before sealing the phone, briefly power it on and plug in a USB-C cable to confirm that: The phone charges The cable fits snugly Data connection (if you want to test with a computer) works Power the phone off again once testing is complete.

Step 8 — Reseal the Phone

What to do: If the back cover adhesive is damaged, apply new adhesive around the frame. Press the back cover into place, working around the edges to ensure full contact. Reinsert the SIM tray.
